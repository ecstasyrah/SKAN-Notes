# Information Security Governance

## The Purpose and Principles

The purpose of governance is to make sure that the organization **actively manages** what information security it does. Information security governance generally follows **enterprise or corporate governance processes**.

Corporate governance aims to ensure an organization does what it intends to do, based on these principles:

* **Policies:** Stating what the organization will do based on objectives, risk, and laws.
* **Responsibility:** Allocating people to ensure policies are followed.
* **Reporting and Checking:** Reporting on adherence and utilizing independent checks.
* **Exception Management:** Managing situations when things don't happen as intended.
* **Accountability:** Holding people accountable for exceptions.
* **Review:** Checking policies to ensure they remain appropriate.

---

## Structure and Leadership

At a corporate level, a general policy mandates an information security policy with named, responsible roles who **report** to an enterprise governance function.

Depending on the organization, policy development is the responsibility of the:
* **CIO** (Chief Information Officer)
* **CISO** (Chief Information Security Officer)
* **Chief Risk Officer**

This policy is developed based on input from **risk and compliance specialists**.

---

## The Information Security Committee

Policy implementation and management are undertaken by a **formal committee**, managed by the governance function leader (who handles agendas, minutes, and tracking).

This committee typically involves:
* **Members:** Senior GRC personnel, IT, Legal, and Risk leaders.
* **Duties:** Adopting policies, monitoring compliance, and dealing with exceptions.

---

## The Reality of Compliance

> **No organization actually exists where it will be able to adhere to 100% of its information security policy all of the time.**

Because of this reality, the compliance function must answer an important question:

> **Is the organization doing what it committed to when it approved the information security policy?**

# Exception Management and Compliance Assurance

## Why Deviations Occur

The information security policy defines controls to reduce risk and comply with external obligations.

However, organizations will sometimes have to knowingly diverge from this policy due to:

* Business demands
* New system vulnerabilities
* Staff changes
* Supplier issues
* Day-to-day management challenges

Also, post-incident reviews often highlight controls that were expected to be in place but were not working or failed to prevent a threat actor's progress.

---

## Variance and Exception Management

Compliance analysts check and monitor these issues to discover **variances or deviations** from policy.

Each deviation could mean the organization is:

* Operating outside of its risk tolerance
* Not compliant with a regulatory or contractual obligation

Because of this, **variance or exception management** is a major function of GRC.

> **If the organization falls out of compliance or operates outside its risk tolerance, it must be authorized by someone with the appropriate internal authority.**

---

## The Review Process

The non-compliance must be worked out and quantified by both **compliance specialists** and **risk specialists**.

Together, this assessment is formally reviewed, typically delegated to a **subcommittee of the information security committee**.

The goal is not to tie the organization in processes, but to ensure that any resulting risks (safety, financial, regulatory, reputational, or contractual) are:

* Formally managed
* Not ignored
* Not accepted by someone without the authorized level of responsibility

### Managing Accepted Risks

In some cases, the committee authorizes resources for an urgent fix. If an exception is outside the risk tolerance but is accepted, it is:

* Recorded in the **risk register**
* Given a **strict lifetime**

A key task of a **risk manager** is to check the risk register for accepted risks that should have been rectified by a certain date. If they haven't, they are brought back to the committee.

> **If an out-of-tolerance risk keeps getting accepted, senior management needs to be aware of it.**

---

## Assurance Activities

The compliance function in GRC undertakes activities to provide assurance that policy controls are in place and working.

These activities include:

* Collating reports from security administration and operations
* Independent testing of controls by the compliance team

When non-compliances are found, a **remediation plan** is usually agreed upon to restore the control within a limited time. Compliance analysts monitor this plan to make sure fixes happen within agreed periods.

Compliance also has the vital role of reporting information security status to **external parties**, providing periodic attestations to regulators or customers that legal/contractual obligations are met.

---

## Internal and External Audits

### Internal Audits
In larger organizations, the **internal audit department** periodically assesses compliance with internal policies.
* Reports to the executive management of the company
* Provides independent assurance that information security is working properly

### External Audits
Used to prove compliance to an external party (at the request of a regulator or customer).
* Can happen periodically, on a random basis, or only after an incident
* These are the tools regulators and customers use to make sure an organization "isn't speeding"

The compliance team often acts as a **liaison** between internal/external auditors and relevant security teams, and tracks the remediation of any issues found.

---

## Governance Recap

The governance function is responsible for:

1. **Policy Creation:** Ensuring the organization has an information security policy based on risk and external obligations.
2. **Oversight:** Making sure requirements are properly implemented and maintained by architecture, engineering, administration, and operations teams (by receiving reports, metrics, and compliance assurance).
3. **Managing Exceptions:** Ensuring variances are properly managed and accepted by someone with the appropriate level of authority.
4. **Reporting:** Reporting on the health of information security to a corporate or enterprise risk and governance function.
