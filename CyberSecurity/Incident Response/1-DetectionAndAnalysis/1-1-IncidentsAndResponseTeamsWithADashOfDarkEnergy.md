## What Is an Incident?

- In general, an incident is an occurrence or situation that may require a response.
- This course focuses on cybersecurity incidents and the technical work needed to assess and respond to them.

### Incident Response Under Pressure

- Incidents can create tense, stressful situations involving financial losses, disrupted operations, and sometimes risks to people’s safety.
- Responders must interpret technical information and make decisions under pressure.

### What Shapes the Response?

- The type and severity of the incident.
- How well the organization and its business functions have prepared.
- The skills, decisions, and coordination of the response team.

### Your Role as an Incident Responder

- Act as a hands-on technical member of the incident response team.
- Ask questions, investigate available information, and help determine what is happening.
- Support initial triage and the early stages of the response.

### What This Course Covers

- How cyber incident response teams are organized and their roles defined.
- The responsibilities of a technical incident responder.
- How to perform initial triage and other early incident response processes.

## Types of Incidents and IR Teams

- This course focuses on systems compromised by threat actors and the technical teams responsible for investigating, containing, and recovering from those compromises.

### Emergency and Cybersecurity Response Teams

| Team | Focus |
|---|---|
| Emergency Response Team (ERT) / Incident Response Team (IRT) | Specialized responses to emergencies such as chemical spills, crashes, or disease outbreaks |
| Computer Incident Response Team (CIRT) | Cybersecurity incidents, such as ransomware or attacks on industrial systems |

- Cyber incidents can affect critical services, including banking, oil supply, and industrial control systems (ICS), including SCADA systems.

### External Incident Response Teams

- Third-party specialists brought in to support an organization after a breach or compromise.
- They may initially lack system access, knowledge of the network, and familiarity with the organization’s tools.
- Available access and background information depend on prior arrangements.
- The course places you in the role of an external technical responder.

### Internal Incident Response Teams

- Responders working within the affected organization.
- Large organizations may maintain dedicated, full-time CIRTs, sometimes operating globally.
- Other organizations assemble an incident response team from existing staff when needed.

### SOC and CIRT Responsibilities

| Function | Typical Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Security Operations Center (SOC) | Continuous monitoring, detection, alert investigation, and escalation |
| CIRT | Investigating confirmed incidents, containing threats, and supporting recovery |

- Responders may work within the SOC’s tiered escalation process or belong to a separate team.
- A CIRT may need additional information about the original detection.
- Response tools, endpoint agents, and access may not cover every affected system.

### Events, Alerts, and Incidents

- Definitions and escalation criteria vary by organization.
- For this course, the instructor uses the following simplified distinction:

| Term | Course Meaning |
|---|---|
| Event / Alert | Activity requiring investigation by a security analyst |
| Incident | Confirmed threat actor activity escalated by management for incident response |

- Large monitoring environments generate many events and alerts; only some require incident response activation.
- Third-party involvement is part of this course’s scenario, not a requirement for every incident.

### Response Across the Organization

| Response Level | Main Focus |
|---|---|
| Strategic | Public and employee communications, organizational priorities, and major business decisions |
| Operational | Maintaining business functions and coordinating day-to-day actions |
| Tactical | Technical investigation, containment, remediation, and recovery |

- Incident response involves the entire organization, not only the security team.
- Technical findings help leaders understand the attack, its impact, and recovery options.

### Course Focus: Tactical Response

- Perform hands-on technical work to determine what happened, stop the attack, and reduce the chance of recurrence.

1. Investigate the attacker’s activity and affected systems.
2. Assess the scope and impact.
3. Contain the compromise.
4. Support remediation and recovery.
5. Address the conditions that could allow the incident to happen again.

- The course emphasizes technical response rather than executive tabletop exercises or media-response practice.
